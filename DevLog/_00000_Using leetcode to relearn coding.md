# Using leetcode to relearn coding.

## Intro

That may be a somewhat untrue title. I can still code, but my final year at university had an unexpected side effect: it left me feeling less confident in my programming abilities than when I started. I am also a believer that learning does not stop the minute that you leave education and it should just be the absolute minimum and I intend to become as competent in my field as possible.

The course offered at my university was a games development degree and it was great at getting you to have a general overview of all the different areas of game development from art to design to coding, but frankly that last column was a bit of an afterthought. We did code in game engines to get mechanics working for both personal and group project games, but we were not strictly taught any specific algorithms, nor was their mastery a large part of the marking criteria for the course. In the first couple of years this was not a handicap as the work load was small enough for me to pursue this knowledge outside of the course, but the high workload of the last year meant a big degradation in this knowledge pool which i hope to rectify.

A major part of this is regaining familiarity of the C++ language. Since our course use Unity primarily C++ was rarely taught despite being the primary language of game development engineers and C# was heavily favoured.

Now its coming time to find a job and there are bills to pay and its time to get back into those algorithms and where better to start than Leetcode. 


As an adult student i have a lot of outside responsibilities in life (and a finite pool of energy). So I am attempting to create a maintainable system of learning whilst I build portfolio and interview skills, because to me if I can hit multiple markers at once its happy days. So like a good programmer I am trying to optimise my cognitive capacity for long term high quality output. One such thing is high cognitive output and concentration only lasts for about 4 hours a day, and thats being generous, most engineers only work on highly cognitive work such as code for only a few hours a day with the rest of the work being admin, communication and medium level tasks. Additionally according to ultradian rythms it hard to concentrate past 90 minutes at a time on complex problems so each work session is broken into 80 minutes which is on the upper end but has some leeway, this prevents the Pomodoro coding problem of having too many breaks for deep work but does not cause you to burn out too quickly.


## The proposed routine  

So my daily routine at the moment consists of:

- 3 heavy coding blocks
- 1 medium reflection block
- 1 admin and blogging block

20 minute move breaks between them for better blood flow and to maintain concentration better.

The three heavy coding blocks are currently going to be 3 sessions of pure answering leetcode questions. I am doing this as previously stated to regain skills, learn and get ready for interviews. This will be for the next 5 weeks when I intend to start looking for work. At this phase I will switch to doing one block of leetcode a day to maintain and two blocks of working on a game project or contributing to open source for some professional experience. The leetcode questions will also periodically involve redoing key concept questions for better deep learning and to really entrench key algorithms.

The medium reflection will always be a write up of what was learned on the day or key things to note or possibly even touch ups to code and commenting for clarity. Whilst doing leetcode this will be written as an attached document to each answered question explaining the reasoning behind the answer along with other thoughts, musing and potential answers.

The light sessions will be admin related tasks such as dealing with potential job emails, related reading or when I have the time I will be attempting regular blog posts on key concepts and algorithms as the third way to cement that knowledge and possibly to help other coders to understand.

I think this is a reasonable routine to keep in touch with several aspects of development and not lead to burnout and keep morale raised in a job hunting scenario... besides what game dev would not want to have time to game!
